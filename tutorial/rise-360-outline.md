# Curriculum Map Builder Tutorial: Rise 360 Outline

A short course, built in Articulate Rise 360, that teaches teachers how to plan a year-long curriculum map with the Curriculum Map Builder. Your Earth and Space Science map is the running example throughout.

## Course at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Audience** | K–12 teachers and curriculum writers, especially new teachers and teachers writing a course for the first time |
| **Length** | About 25–30 minutes, in 8 lessons |
| **Format** | Rise 360 blocks lessons, with knowledge checks and a final quiz |
| **Running example** | Earth and Space Science (grades 9–12, 32 weeks, 6 units) |
| **Tool** | https://tools.waterdog.institute |
| **Final product** | Each learner leaves with the first unit of their own map, saved as a map file |

### Course objectives

By the end, learners can:

1. Explain what a curriculum map is for and why it starts from a few priority standards.
2. Set up a course and lay out its units and weeks.
3. Pick a key standard for a unit and separate it from the supporting standards.
4. Fill in a unit's big idea, essential question, knowledge, skills, learning activities and assessment.
5. Save, reopen and share a map, and export it as a PDF.

## Lesson 1: Why map a curriculum?

*Goal: learners understand the purpose of a map and the idea of priority standards before they touch the tool.* (3 minutes)

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| **Text (Heading + paragraph)** | A curriculum map is the whole year on a few pages: what students learn, in what order, and how you'll know they learned it. |
| **Statement** | "You can't teach every standard to mastery. A good map decides which ones matter most." |
| **Accordion** | Three things a map does: **shows the sequence** (how the year builds), **sets priorities** (which standards get the most time), **lines up assessment** (every unit ends with evidence of learning). |
| **Image** | Page 1 of the Earth and Space Science PDF, the Year at a glance. Caption: the whole year, Big Bang to human impact, on one page. |
| **Text** | Introduce the big ideas and essential questions from backward design (Wiggins and McTighe, *Understanding by Design*). You start from what students should understand, then plan backward. |
| **Knowledge check (multiple choice)** | *What is the main job of a curriculum map?* Correct: show the year's sequence, priorities and assessment at a glance. Distractors: replace daily lesson plans; list every activity; grade students. |

## Lesson 3: Set up the course and units

*Goal: learners create a course and its units.* (3 minutes)

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| **Process** | 1. Fill in the **Course** box: course name, grade, length, standards, author, description. 2. Add units with **+ Add unit**. 3. Give each unit a **title** and **weeks**. 4. Reorder with **Move up / Move down**. 5. Fold what you're not working on. |
| **Image** | The Earth and Space Science course box. |
| **Image** | All six units folded: "Unit 1 · The Big Bang and the Beginning of Our Universe · 4 weeks"… |
| **Text** | Tips for keeping a long map manageable: click any header to fold it, use **Collapse all** and **Expand all**, and each unit has five sections that fold on their own: Overview, Knowledge, Skills, Learning activities and Assessment. Units start folded when you come back. |
| **Text** | Sequencing tip: order units so each one builds on the last. Earth and Space Science follows the story of matter, from the Big Bang to the forming of elements, Earth's beginning, plate tectonics, Earth's systems, and people. |
| **Knowledge check (multiple choice)** | *You want to move Unit 4 before Unit 3. What do you do?* Correct: use Move up on Unit 4. |

## Lesson 4: Big idea, standards and essential question

*Goal: learners can write a unit's Overview, including choosing one key standard.* (5 minutes)

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| **Text** | The **big idea** is the one understanding students should keep after the unit. Example: "As humans have created tools to view the universe, we can better see into its past and expand our understanding of its beginning." |
| **Tabs** | **Key standard:** the one standard the unit teaches to mastery. It's highlighted on the map and appears in the Year at a glance. **Standards:** the other standards the unit touches. Example: Unit 1's key standard is HS-ESS1-2; HS-PS4-1 and the NM standard HS-SS-1 support it. |
| **Sorting activity** | Learners sort the five standards from Units 1 and 2 (HS-ESS1-2, HS-PS4-1, HS-SS-1 NM, HS-ESS1-3, HS-ESS1-1) into **Key standard** or **Supporting standard** for their unit. |
| **Multimedia (GIF)** | Pasting `HS-ESS1-2 - Construct an explanation of the Big Bang theory…` into the code box and watching it split into code and text. |
| **Text** | The **essential question** is an open question the whole unit works toward answering. Type one per line. Example: "How did the universe begin and how does evidence improve our understanding?" |
| **Flashcard grid** | Front: a question. Back: is it a good essential question, and why? Mix strong examples ("What moves Earth's surface and how do we know it moves?") with weak ones ("What is a plate boundary?", too narrow and factual). |
| **Knowledge check (multiple response)** | *Which belong in Standards rather than Key standard?* |

## Lesson 5: Knowledge and skills

*Goal: learners can list what students will know and be able to do.* (4 minutes)

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| **Text** | **Knowledge** is what students understand. **Skills** are what they can do. Keeping them separate makes it clear what you're actually teaching. |
| **Accordion** | **Key concepts:** each has a name and a short description, added with **+ Add concept**. **Vocabulary:** type terms separated by commas, and each becomes a bullet on the map. |
| **Multimedia (GIF)** | Typing `Big Bang theory, galaxy, redshift, spectrum` and the bullets appearing in the map. |
| **Labeled graphic** | The skill type dropdown, with the four types: **Receptive** (taking in information, like observing or reading data), **Processing** (working with it, like evaluating evidence or modeling), **Expressive** (communicating it, like constructing an argument), **Physical** (hands-on, like using a spectroscope). Plus **Custom…** for your own type. |
| **Sorting activity** | Learners sort eight Earth and Space Science skills into the four types. Example: "Using a Bunsen burner" goes under Physical. |
| **Text** | On the map, skills are grouped by type, and types with no skills are left out. |
| **Knowledge check (multiple choice)** | *"Constructing an argument" is which type of skill?* Correct: Expressive. |

## Lesson 6: Learning activities and assessment

*Goal: learners can plan activities and line every objective up with evidence.* (4 minutes)

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| **Text** | **Learning activities** are the labs, investigations and projects in the unit, each with a name and a description. Example: "Spectroscope lab: students view hydrogen, helium and neon gas tubes and identify elements in a star's light." |
| **Process** | Assessment, step by step: 1. Write a **formative objective** (what students practice). 2. Pair it with the **artifact** that shows it (what students produce). 3. Add more pairs with **+ Add formative pair**. 4. Write one **summative objective and artifact** for the end of the unit. |
| **Image** | Unit 1's assessment table: Wave relationships / Wave lab data table, and so on, ending in "Explaining the universe's beginning / Written scientific explanation". |
| **Statement** | "Every objective needs evidence. If you can't name the artifact, students can't show you they learned it." |
| **Matching (knowledge check)** | Match four Unit 3 objectives to their artifacts. Example: Dating rocks → Half-life graph. |
| **Text** | The curriculum map shows only the titles of objectives and artifacts, to keep the map readable. Detailed descriptions belong in your unit plans. |

## Lesson 7: Year at a glance, saving and exporting

*Goal: learners can review their year and get it out of the tool.* (3 minutes)

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| **Image** | The Year at a glance table. It builds itself from each unit's title, weeks, key standard and essential question, and adds up the total weeks. |
| **Text** | Use it to check the sequence: does each unit build on the last? Does the total fit your school year? |
| **Tabs** | **Save map:** downloads a `.json` file of your map. Use it to back up your work, move between computers, or share with colleagues. **Open map:** loads a saved file to keep editing. **Download PDF:** a clean, searchable PDF with the Year at a glance on page 1 and each unit after it. It always prints in light colors. **Print:** the browser's print window, if you want its options. |
| **Multimedia (attachment)** | The Earth and Space Science PDF, and the map file so learners can open the complete example in the tool. |
| **Knowledge check (multiple choice)** | *You want to keep working on your map at home. What do you do?* Correct: Save map, then Open map at home. |

## Lesson 8: Build your first unit (practice and wrap-up)

*Goal: learners apply everything to their own course.* (5 minutes, plus their own work time)

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| **Text** | Your turn. Open the tool in a new tab and build the first unit of a course you teach. |
| **Process (checklist)** | Course details → Unit 1 title and weeks → big idea → one key standard → supporting standards → essential question → 2–3 key concepts and vocabulary → one skill of each type → 2–3 learning activities → formative pairs and one summative pair → check the Year at a glance → **Save map**. |

| **Quiz lesson** | 6–8 questions covering Lessons 1–7. Passing score 80%. |

## Final quiz question bank

1. What is the difference between the key standard and the other standards in a unit?
2. Which is the better essential question: "What is a galaxy?" or "How did the universe begin and how does evidence improve our understanding?" Why?
3. You type `fusion, star, supernova` in Vocabulary. What appears on the map?
4. Which skill type is "Evaluating evidence"? (Processing)
5. What two things make up a formative pair? (An objective and the artifact that shows it)
6. Where does the Year at a glance get its information? (Each unit's title, weeks, key standard and essential question)
7. How do you keep a copy of your map you can open on another computer? (Save map)
8. True or false: your map is uploaded to a server when you download the PDF. (False, everything runs in your browser)
